# Direct Anime Shorts

1. Read the active pipeline README and episode config. Preserve the episode's asset policy and cite any external assets. Keep existing renders and source projects recoverable.
2. Define the opening question, character intention, reversal, and payoff. Build a frame-indexed shot plan with framing, action, focal point, offscreen direction, sound cues, and cut points. Give each shot a reason to change.
3. Block the setup, anticipation, contact, and recovery as silhouettes. Choose the engine according to the asset and movement. Use layered 2D meshes for controlled planes, Three.js for skinned cel characters or 2.5D stage depth, or Blender for complex 3D lighting and deformation. Preserve the existing Canvas2D path for blocking and compatible episodes.
4. Inspect a small draft at phone size. Look at key poses and their neighboring frames, including the first and last frames of cuts. Correct readability, ground contact, eye lines, mattes, or UI collisions before spending time on polish.
5. Render the intended delivery settings and verify exact frames, A/V timing, source-to-master color, decode, and peak headroom. Review the final with sound and report what the evidence supports.

Load only the guides needed for the shot.

- [Action and editing](references/action-and-editing.md) for pose timing, smears, contact, sound, and loop closure.

# Direct Anime Shorts

1. Read the active pipeline README and episode config. **Never use generative AI image or video tools**, including for sprites, sprite sheets, textures, backgrounds or individual frames. Use authored procedural visuals, manually made art or licensed human-created assets; cite external sources. Render genuine animation, not a static slideshow. Keep existing renders and source projects recoverable.
2. Define the opening question, character intention, reversal, and payoff. Build a frame-indexed shot plan with framing, action, focal point, offscreen direction, sound cues, and cut points. Give each shot a reason to change.
3. Block the setup, anticipation, contact, and recovery as silhouettes. Choose the engine according to the asset and movement. Use layered 2D meshes for controlled planes, Three.js for skinned cel characters or 2.5D stage depth, or Blender for complex 3D lighting and deformation. Prefer licensed rigged VRM/GLB characters or layered Rigby2D animation for hero close-ups. Keep the geometric Canvas2D path for blocking and compatible episodes rather than presenting it as premium character animation.
4. Inspect a small draft at phone size. Look at key poses and their neighboring frames, including the first and last frames of cuts. Correct readability, ground contact, eye lines, mattes, or UI collisions before spending time on polish.
5. Render the intended delivery settings and verify exact frames, A/V timing, source-to-master color, decode, and peak headroom. Review the final with sound and report what the evidence supports.

For specialist support follow [skill-pack routing](../../../docs/ANIMATION_SKILLS.md): motion design for movement/composition, animated shorts for story/2D-in-3D staging, Blender for Blender-only craft. The episode brief and AnimationLab renderer contract override any third-party recipe.
